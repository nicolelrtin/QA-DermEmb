# Assessing Dermal Lesions: Reference on Established and Current Practice

*Scope: qualitative and semi-quantitative clinical assessment, quantitative imaging and AI, and computer-graphics models of skin appearance as they bear on lesion assessment. Literature through October 2026; 131 unique sources (130 with DOIs; Ware 2020 in *Cutis* has no registered DOI). Takeaways were written from PubMed and arXiv abstracts. Sources marked* metadata_only *in the appendix contribute no numbers.*

## Bottom line

1. **Lesion description is set by consensus, not measurement.** The ILDS glossary ([Nast 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjd.14419)) and the IDS dermoscopy terminology ([Kittler 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2015.12.038)) are the standard vocabularies. Agreement on overall diagnosis is fair to good, but agreement on individual dermoscopic criteria is poor (ICC about 0.4; [Argenziano 2003](https://doi.org/10.1067/mjd.2003.281); [Carrera 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2016.0624)). Labels at the level of individual features are therefore much noisier than diagnosis labels.
2. **Scoring algorithms trade specificity for sensitivity, and they mainly help non-experts.** The ABCD/TDS, 7-point, Menzies, CASH and 3-point checklists reach roughly 85–98% sensitivity, with specificity from 25% to 75%. In head-to-head testing their AUCs are similar ([Carrera 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2016.0624)). The Cochrane reviews found no clear accuracy gain over expert pattern analysis.
3. **Dermoscopy is the evidence-based standard adjunct.** Compared with naked-eye inspection, it raises the diagnostic odds about 4.7-fold for melanoma ([Dinnes 2018b](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011902.pub2)) and about 8.2-fold for BCC ([Dinnes 2018c](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011901.pub2)). In-person assessment outperforms assessment from images ([Dinnes 2018a](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013194)).
4. **Primary-care triage now uses simplified "malignant or not" algorithms**: TADA (sensitivity 94.8%, specificity 72.3%; [Rogers 2016](https://doi.org/10.3122/jabfm.2016.06.160079)) and chaos-and-clues ([Rosendahl 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2010.03.039)).
5. **Change over time is the main measurable signal in practice.** Sequential digital dermoscopy works at intervals of at least about 4.5 months ([Kittler 2006](https://doi.org/10.1001/archderm.142.9.1113); [Salerni 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.12032)). The first RCT of 3D total-body photography combined with sequential dermoscopy increased excisions without improving melanoma detection at one year ([Soyer 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2025.0211)).
6. **Among the adjunct devices, RCM has the strongest evidence.** It has Cochrane-level accuracy data ([Dinnes 2018d](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013190)) and RCT evidence of fewer unnecessary excisions ([Pellacani 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2022.1570)). OCT and LC-OCT are mainly validated for BCC. The point-probe spectroscopies (EIS, Raman, ESS/DermaSensor) all have a rule-out profile: sensitivity about 96–100% with specificity about 15–36%.
7. **AI matches experts in retrospective reader studies but is brittle when deployed.** A meta-analysis put pooled sensitivity/specificity at 87.0%/77.1% ([Salinas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-024-01103-x)). In ISIC 2019, 47.1% of images from categories absent from training were called malignant ([Combalia 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500%2822%2900021-8)). In a prospective comparison, an algorithm that had outperformed specialists in experiments did worse than them ([Menzies 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500%2823%2900130-9)). Foundation models (PanDerm, [Yan 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03747-y); MONET, [Kim 2024a](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-02887-x)) are the 2024–2026 frontier, but independent validation is limited.
8. **Skin tone is a measurement problem as well as a fairness problem.** Fitzpatrick type is a sunburn scale, not a measure of colour. The Monk scale and colorimetric ITA are better descriptors, but published ITA cut-offs for "dark" skin are too light ([Lipnick 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjd/ljaf397); [Weir 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-02245-2)). Both AI and dermatologists perform worse on dark skin ([Daneshjou 2022a](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq6147)). Melanin also physically masks the haemoglobin signal in visible light ([Pryor 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jid.2026.03.025)).
9. **Some computer-graphics work is directly relevant, but only in specific places.** Physically based skin models (dipole/multipole BSSRDF, layered spectral chromophore models, MCML) and RGB melanin/haemoglobin decomposition give interpretable, physics-grounded features and controllable simulation of imaging conditions. SIAscopy is the clinical translation of this work, and it has not displaced dermoscopy ([Glud 2009](https://doi.org/10.1097/cmr.0b013e328322fe5f)). Microgeometry and appearance-capture work targets photorealism of normal skin and has weak relevance to lesion assessment. Synthetic lesion data are most useful for augmenting rare classes and skin tones and for stress-testing models ([Ktena 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-02838-6)), not as a proven way to raise clinical performance.

## Part I. Clinical assessment

### Morphology & terminology

The descriptive vocabulary for lesions is settled by consensus rather than by data. The ILDS glossary ([Nast 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjd.14419)) fixes 13 basic primary-lesion terms plus over 100 modifiers through Delphi voting. Dermoscopy has two co-existing lexicons: metaphoric terms (e.g. 'spoke-wheel') and descriptive terms (e.g. lines, dots, clods). The IDS consensus ([Kittler 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2015.12.038)) keeps both, provides a mapping between them, and discourages new metaphors. The weak point is reproducibility. The Consensus Net Meeting ([Argenziano 2003](https://doi.org/10.1067/mjd.2003.281)) found fair-to-good agreement on overall diagnosis but poor agreement on most individual dermoscopic criteria. For ML work this means that per-feature labels are much noisier than diagnosis labels, and that vocabularies need explicit definitions. Open gaps: there is little validation of either terminology across skin tones, and no machine-readable standard ontology exists that links ILDS clinical terms to IDS dermoscopic terms.

### Melanocytic rules/algorithms

Two families of rules exist. Naked-eye rules include ABCD ([Friedman 1985](https://doi.org/10.3322/canjclin.35.3.130)), later extended to ABCDE with 'evolving' ([Abbasi 2004](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.292.22.2771)), and the Glasgow 7-point checklist ([MacKie 1990](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.301.6759.1005)), whose weighted version was validated in primary care ([Walter 2013](https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp13X667213): 91.7% sensitivity, 53.4% specificity for melanoma at cut-off 4). Dermoscopic scoring systems include the ABCD rule/TDS ([Nachbar 1994](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0190-9622%2894%2970061-3)), the 7-point checklist ([Argenziano 1998](https://doi.org/10.1001/archderm.134.12.1563)), Menzies ([Menzies 1996](https://doi.org/10.1001/archderm.1996.03890340038007)), CASH ([Henning 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2006.09.003)) and the 3-point checklist ([Soyer 2004](https://doi.org/10.1159/000075042)). Each typically buys high sensitivity at the cost of specificity, and in head-to-head testing their AUCs are similar ([Carrera 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2016.0624)). The ugly duckling sign ([Grob 1998](https://doi.org/10.1001/archderm.134.1.103-a)) moves the reference point from absolute thresholds to comparison with the patient's own other nevi. In a multi-reader study this comparison raised specificity and reduced biopsies 6.9-fold ([Gaudy-Marqueste 2017](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2016.5500)). Contested: the Cochrane review found no clear accuracy gain from algorithms over expert gestalt ([Dinnes 2018d](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013190)). Consensus is that algorithms mainly help novices and teaching, while experts rely on pattern analysis. Gap: almost all derivation cohorts were fair-skinned populations with truncal nevi.

### Non-melanocytic & triage algorithms

Triage algorithms ask 'malignant or not' rather than 'melanoma or not'. They target primary care, where BCC and SCC dominate. TADA ([Rogers 2016](https://doi.org/10.3122/jabfm.2016.06.160079): 94.8% sensitivity, 72.3% specificity even among untrained users) and chaos and clues ([Rosendahl 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2010.03.039): 98.6% sensitivity for BCC, 86.5% for pigmented SCC, 79.3% for melanoma) rely on a few high-yield clues: architectural disorder, blue-grey structures, shiny white structures, atypical vessels and ulceration. BLINCK adds patient concern and change, but rests on a 50-lesion, four-reader study ([Bourne 2012](https://doi.org/10.5826/dpc.0202a12)). Lesion-specific criteria are well established for pigmented BCC ([Menzies 2000](https://doi.org/10.1001/archderm.136.8.1012): no network plus ovoid nests, leaf-like areas, spoke-wheels or arborizing vessels; 97% sensitivity) and for the AK to Bowen disease to SCC continuum ([Zalaudek 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2011.02.011)). AK clinical thickness grading I-III is conventionally attributed to [Olsen 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0190-9622%2891%2970113-g), but that abstract shows only lesion counts, so the grade definition is metadata_only here. Gaps: validation of SCC criteria is retrospective and lacks sensitivity/specificity figures, and the 2024 EADO AK guideline ([Kandolf 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.19897)) still treats diagnosis as clinical, using dermatoscopy, RCM or OCT as adjuncts.

### Diagnostic accuracy & agreement

The 2018 Cochrane DTA series is the anchor. Visual inspection alone misses melanomas (sensitivity 76.7% and specificity 95.7% for in-person excision decisions), and in-person assessment beats assessment from images (relative diagnostic odds ratio 8.5) ([Dinnes 2018a](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013194)). Adding dermoscopy raises the diagnostic odds about 4.7-fold for melanoma ([Dinnes 2018b](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011902.pub2)) and 8.2-fold for BCC ([Dinnes 2018c](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011901.pub2)), consistent with [Vestergaard 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2133.2008.08713.x). Teledermatology classifies most malignancies correctly but over-refers ([Chuchu 2018a](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013193)). Consumer AI apps performed poorly, with sensitivity from 7% to 73% ([Chuchu 2018b](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013192); [Freeman 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m127)). A 2024 meta-analysis found AI comparable to expert dermatologists and better than generalists ([Salinas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-024-01103-x)), but these are mostly retrospective reader studies. Inter-observer agreement on individual criteria stays poor (ICC around 0.4; [Carrera 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2016.0624)). Open issues: verification bias (only excised lesions get histology), spectrum effects that differ between primary and secondary care, the gap between image-based and in-person performance, and the scarcity of prospective real-world AI-assisted trials.

### Skin of color

Fitzpatrick skin type measures sunburn propensity, not skin colour or race, yet it is routinely misused as both ([Ware 2020](https://doi.org/none registered %28see PMID%29)). In a prospective comparison against colorimetry, the Monk Skin Tone scale was more repeatable and more informative for AI fairness auditing, and colour values extracted from dermoscopic images were poorly calibrated ([Weir 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-02245-2)). Commentators now advocate Monk adoption ([Mehta 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2025.05.1437)). Disease presentation differs in skin of colour. In Black patients, melanoma is predominantly acral or lower-extremity, and 41.8% present at stage III-IV ([Fernandez 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/pcmr.13065)), which ABCD rules built on truncal nevi do not capture. Dermoscopic evidence in skin of colour is mostly case reports, concentrated in phototype IV ([Enechukwu 2023](https://doi.org/10.5826/dpc.1304S1a308S)). Textbooks under-represent dark skin ([Adelekun 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2020.04.084)), and both AI models and dermatologists perform worse on dark skin ([Daneshjou 2022a](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq6147)). A 2026 spectral analysis shows that melanin masks the haemoglobin signal, so detecting redness in visible light is physically harder in darker skin ([Pryor 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jid.2026.03.025)). That point bears directly on photograph-based phenotyping. Gaps: there are no validated morphology vocabularies for erythema or violaceous tones in darker skin, and no prospective accuracy studies stratified by objectively measured skin tone.

### Monitoring, measurement & guidelines

Monitoring over time is the main semi-quantitative tool in practice. Sequential digital dermoscopy picks up melanomas that have no specific features, through asymmetric growth and structural change, but intervals under 4.5 months are not discriminative ([Kittler 2006](https://doi.org/10.1001/archderm.142.9.1113)). A meta-analysis of more than 52,000 monitored lesions found that yield rises with follow-up duration, with a number needed to monitor of about 348 ([Salerni 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.12032)). The first RCT of 3D total-body photography plus SDDI by teledermatology in high-risk patients increased excisions and keratinocyte-cancer detection but not melanoma detection ([Soyer 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2025.0211)). This tempers enthusiasm for TBP without AI. Current guidelines endorse dermatoscopy and structured surveillance: EADO melanoma 2024 ([Garbe 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2024.115152), valid to end of 2026), AAD primary melanoma ([Swetter 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2018.08.055), still the current version as far as this PubMed search found), EADO AK ([Kandolf 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.19897)) and cSCC ([Stratigos 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2026.116763)), and the IDS lentigo maligna consensus ([Forsea 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.70406)). Gaps: lesion size and change are rarely measured in standardised units, there is no consensus protocol for TBP image acquisition, and NICE NG14/NG12 guidance is not PubMed-indexed and was not retrieved here.

## Part II. Quantitative imaging and AI

### Non-invasive quantitative imaging

Established: each adjunct modality trades depth, resolution and specificity differently. RCM is the best-evidenced, with Cochrane pooled sensitivity ~92% and much higher specificity than dermoscopy in equivocal lesions ([Dinnes 2018d](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013190)), and is the only modality here with RCT evidence of fewer unnecessary excisions (NNE 5.3 -> 3.0; [Pellacani 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2022.1570)). OCT has good evidence only for BCC ([Ferrante di Ruffano 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013189)); LC-OCT, which adds cellular resolution to OCT depth, raises specificity over dermoscopy at equal sensitivity, again mainly for BCC ([Cinotti 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics13030361); [Mtimet 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.20459)). HFUS tracks Breslow thickness well only at >=20 MHz and for tumours >0.75 mm ([Sellyn 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.20179)). The point-probe spectroscopies (EIS/Nevisense, Raman, ESS/DermaSensor) and hyperspectral imaging share one profile: very high sensitivity (~96-100%) with low specificity (~26-36%) ([Malvehy 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjd.13121); [Lui 2012](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-11-4061); [Hosking 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/lsm.23055); [Manolakos 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdin.2023.08.019)), i.e. rule-out tools whose PPV depends on the referral population. DermaSensor's 2024 FDA authorization is the first for an AI device used by non-specialists ([Venkatesh 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-024-01161-1)).

Contested/open: most accuracy data come from expert centres and lesions already selected for biopsy, which inflates sensitivity and hides real-world specificity. Skin colour itself is a measured quantity: ITA from L*a*b* colorimetry ([Chardon 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-2494.1991.tb00561.x)) is the main objective metric, but a 2026 cohort shows that published ITA cut-offs for 'dark' skin are too light and that race, Fitzpatrick and Monk scales classify the same people very differently ([Lipnick 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjd/ljaf397)), so the choice of skin-tone metric affects fairness analyses. 3D total body photography gives repeatable lesion-level data (R=0.89), but automated naevus counting and CNN specificity are not yet clinically adequate ([Cerminara 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2023.112954)); annotation protocols that extract quantitative skin phenotypes (colour, UV damage, naevus distribution) are under way ([Primiero 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2024.1380984)).

### Computational image analysis & standards

Established: classical pipelines operationalize clinician heuristics as hand-crafted features. The ABCD rule weights asymmetry, border, colour and dermoscopic structures into a score (threshold 5.45; [Nachbar 1994](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0190-9622%2894%2970061-3)), and computer vision pipelines followed the segmentation, feature extraction (shape/colour/texture such as GLCM) and classification sequence ([Celebi 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/JBHI.2019.2895803)). The 2016-2017 ISIC challenges provided shared benchmarks ([Codella 2018](https://doi.org/10.1109/isbi.2018.8363547)), and deep segmentation now dominates, though artefacts (hair, bubbles) and acquisition variability remain the main failure sources ([Mirikharaji 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2023.102863)). Acquisition standards come from the ISIC Delphi consensus, with 33 recommendations on lighting, background, scale, colour calibration and other items ([Katragadda 2017](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2016.3949)), shown to be practically achievable ([Finnane 2017](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2016.6214)). DICOM is argued to be the right container because it carries patient, study and acquisition metadata ([Caffery 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10278-017-0045-8)), and open tooling for converting dermatology images to DICOM exists ([Eapen 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10278-022-00636-5)).

Gaps: adoption of colour calibration and DICOM in routine dermatology remains limited, so most public images lack calibrated colour and acquisition metadata. This directly limits quantitative colorimetric or textural readouts from ordinary photographs. Few recent primary studies validate hand-crafted features against histology or physical measurement; they survive mainly as interpretable concepts (see MONET in theme 3). A dedicated, verified GLCM primary reference was not included (see deviations).

### AI/deep learning classification

Established: reader studies from 2017-2020 showed CNNs matching or beating dermatologists on curated image sets ([Esteva 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature21056); [Haenssle 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/annonc/mdy166): specificity 82.5% vs 71.3% at matched sensitivity; [Tschandl 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045%2819%2930333-X): top algorithms beat experts on 7-class pigmented lesions). Human-AI collaboration beats either alone, but faulty AI misleads even experts ([Tschandl 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-020-0942-0)). A 2024 meta-analysis puts AI at sensitivity/specificity 87.0%/77.1%, comparable to experts and better than generalists ([Salinas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-024-01103-x)). The benchmark ecosystem is HAM10000, SIIM-ISIC 2020 (patient-level context), BCN20000 (hard sites and an OOD class), PAD-UFES-20 (smartphone images with metadata), Fitzpatrick17k and DDI ([Tschandl 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2018.161); [Rotemberg 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-021-00815-z); [Hernández-Pérez 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-03387-w); [Pacheco 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2020.106221); [Groh 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvprw53098.2021.00201); [Daneshjou 2022a](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq6147)).

Contested and changing: performance drops on out-of-distribution data (OOD: images from conditions or sources absent from training). In ISIC 2019, algorithms got only 6% of unseen-category images right and labelled 47% of them malignant ([Combalia 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500%2822%2900021-8)). Prospectively, an experimentally superior algorithm was inferior to specialists ([Menzies 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500%2823%2900130-9)), while primary-care and teledermatology deployments look promising but have few melanomas ([Papachristou 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjd/ljae021); [Marsden 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2024.1302363) for Skin Analytics DERM). Bias is documented at three levels: datasets (ethnicity recorded for 1.3% of public images; [Wen 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500%2821%2900252-1)), models and dermatologists on dark skin ([Daneshjou 2022a](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq6147)), and decision support that raises overall accuracy while widening the skin-tone gap for generalists ([Groh 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02728-3)). In 2024-2025 the field moved to foundation models: PanDerm, multimodal and label-efficient across 28 tasks ([Yan 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03747-y)); MONET, image-text concept scoring for auditing ([Kim 2024a](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-02887-x)); and SkinGPT-4, an LLM-based chat system that has only limited evaluation ([Zhou 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50043-3)). Reporting should follow CLEAR Derm, TRIPOD+AI and CONSORT-AI ([Daneshjou 2022b](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2021.4915); [Collins 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078378); [Liu 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500%2820%2930218-1)). Open gaps: randomized outcome trials of AI, calibrated performance across measured skin tone (ITA rather than Fitzpatrick), and independent validation of foundation models.

## Part III. Computer-graphics representations of skin

### Light transport & chromophore maps

Established: graphics and biomedical optics converged on the same forward-model stack for skin. The dipole diffusion BSSRDF ([Jensen 2001](https://doi.org/10.1145/383259.383319)) was generalized to thin and stacked layers by the multipole model ([Donner 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1073204.1073308)), then to spatially heterogeneous, chromophore-parameterized spectral layers inverted per pixel from multispectral photographs ([Donner 2008](https://doi.org/10.1145/1409060.1409093)); BioSpec ([Krishnaswamy 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2004.00764.x)) and MCML ([Wang 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0169-2607%2895%2901640-f)) provide Monte Carlo ground truth, and Jacques (2013) supplies the standard absorption/scattering parameters for blood, melanin, water and scatterers. On the cheap end, ICA on optical-density RGB separates melanin and hemoglobin maps from ordinary photographs ([Tsumura 1999](https://doi.org/10.1364/josaa.16.002169); [Tsumura 2003](https://doi.org/10.1145/882262.882344)). Clinical relevance is strongest here of all four themes: chromophore maps are physically interpretable features (dermal melanin, blood displacement), and the SIAscope work translated them into diagnosis, with ~80%/83% sensitivity/specificity in 348 lesions ([Claridge 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1361-8415%2803%2900033-1); [Moncrieff 2002](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2133.2002.04569.x), which reports the two figures in swapped order for the same dataset). Contested: an independent prospective comparison found SIAscopy more sensitive (100% vs 92%) but much less specific (59% vs 81%) than dermoscopy ([Glud 2009](https://doi.org/10.1097/cmr.0b013e328322fe5f)), so physics-derived maps have not displaced expert pattern recognition.

Recent change (2020-2025): inversion is moving from lookup tables/iterative fits to neural networks trained on simulated spectra ([Gitlina 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.14055); [Aliaga 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.14887); [Livecchi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.jbo.29.9.093507), >4000x faster than least squares), and graphics models are moving toward heterogeneous volumetric skin ([Wang 2025](https://doi.org/10.26599/cvm.2025.9450360), metadata only). Weaknesses for lesion assessment: most graphics models are validated visually on normal facial skin, some explicitly disclaim diagnostic use ([Iglesias-Guitian 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12540)) or let parameters exceed physiological ranges ([Aliaga 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.14887)); chromophore estimates from consumer cameras are sensitive to illumination assumptions ([Kuzmina 2022](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.jbo.27.2.026004)) and to the two-pigment/linearity assumptions of ICA, which break down in lesions with dermal melanin, keratin, scale or ulceration. Open gaps: physically calibrated inversion on pigmented lesions across Fitzpatrick types with histological ground truth, and uncertainty estimates for per-pixel chromophore maps derived from smartphone images.

### Skin microgeometry, topography & appearance capture

Established: appearance capture separates skin reflectance into specular, single-scatter and shallow/deep subsurface components using controlled illumination and polarization ([Debevec 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/344779.344855) Light Stage, metadata only; [Ghosh 2008](https://doi.org/10.1145/1409060.1409092)), and population-level skin reflectance statistics across 149 subjects vary with age, skin type and external factors ([Weyrich 2006](https://doi.org/10.1145/1141911.1141987)). Microgeometry (pores, fine furrows) is captured at high resolution on small patches and transferred to whole-face scans ([Graham 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12053)); in vivo topography can be obtained by photometric stereo, though wavelength-dependent, non-Lambertian skin BRDF biases reconstructions ([Sohaib 2013](https://doi.org/10.1364/josaa.30.000278)). Clinical/cosmetic devices such as Antera 3D (multidirectional multi-wavelength LED photometry) output roughness, wrinkle, pore, melanin and hemoglobin indices, but agreement with other devices is partial: texture and redness correlate with VISIA, wrinkle and pore metrics do not ([Linming 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/srt.12381)).

Relevance to lesion assessment is moderate to weak. The direct value is (a) polarization/cross-polarization physics shared with dermoscopy, useful for simulating modality differences, and (b) the evidence that surface microrelief governs specular highlights, which matter when inferring texture from photographs. However, graphics microgeometry work targets photorealism of normal facial skin and synthesizes texture by exemplar transfer, not by modelling disease (scale, papillomatosis, regression structures); device topography indices are mostly validated in cosmetology (aging, pores) rather than against histopathology of lesions. Gap: no source retrieved here validates surface topography metrics for discriminating lesion classes; the 2023-2026 literature in this subtheme was sparse in the databases searched (no recent PubMed hits for skin microtopography + deep learning).

### Synthetic lesion data for ML

Two families exist. Learned generators (GANs, then diffusion) synthesize lesion images: DermGAN controlled lesion size, location and skin colour and helped rare malignant classes ([Ghorbani 2019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1911.08716)); style transfer to darker skin raised a small classifier's AUC from 0.63 to 0.72 ([Rezk 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/39143)); text-to-image diffusion augmentation improved overall and underrepresented-group performance on Fitzpatrick 17k ([Sagers 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2211.13352)) and preserved accuracy even when training on fully synthetic data ([Akrout 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-53767-7_10); [Farooq 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/embc53108.2024.10781852)). The strongest evidence is Ktena (2024, Nat Med): diffusion-learned augmentations improved robustness and subgroup fairness in dermatology among three modalities, especially out of distribution. Knowledge/physics-based generators render lesions on 3D bodies (DermSynth3D, [Sinha 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2024.103145)) or simulate layered skin with growing lesions and controllable melanin/blood/hair (S-SYNTH, [Kim 2024b](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-72384-1_69); [Sizikova 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11548-026-03587-2)), giving dense labels and controllable acquisition parameters; their main validated claim is that synthetic test sets reproduce comparative ranking trends seen on real images, i.e. they are tools for controlled evaluation rather than proven performance boosters.

Contested: a careful re-analysis found GAN augmentation and anonymization helped only on out-of-distribution test sets ([Bissoto 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvprw53098.2021.00204)), and a 2025 review of 27 GAN studies found little clinical validation and heterogeneous metrics (FID, SSIM) that do not measure diagnostic fidelity ([Izu-Belloso 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12101113)). Many recent diffusion papers are conference/preprint level with no subgroup statistics in abstracts. Relevance to clinical lesion assessment: genuinely useful for augmentation of rare classes and skin tones, for stress-testing models under simulated lighting/camera/hair conditions, and for generating segmentation labels; weak where synthetic lesions are texture-pasted or recolored (DermSynth3D, [Rezk 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/39143)), since morphology on darker skin is not simply a colour shift and diagnostic features may be hallucinated. Open gaps: prospective or external-test evidence that synthetic data improve clinical sensitivity in underrepresented groups, dermatologist-validated diagnostic fidelity of generated morphology, and memorization/privacy auditing of diffusion models trained on patient images.

### 3D skin/lesion reconstruction & digital twins

3D total-body photography (3D TBP: multi-camera stereophotogrammetry producing a textured body mesh) is the mature form of 3D skin reconstruction in dermatology. Evidence: CNN naevus counting on 3D TBP reached kappa 0.56 (>=2 mm) to 0.72 (>=5 mm) vs a dermatologist ([Betz-Stablein 2022](https://doi.org/10.1159/000517218)); in a real-world high-risk cohort, a 3D-TBP CNN reached sensitivity 90%, specificity 64.6%, AUC 0.92, matching dermatologist sensitivity but with lower specificity and badly inflated automated naevus counts ([Cerminara 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2023.112954)). The 2025 RCT ([Soyer 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2025.0211), n=314) found that adding 3D TBP plus sequential dermoscopy via teledermatology increased excisions per person (5.73 vs 3.99) without significantly reducing melanoma rate at one year, so 3D imaging per se has not yet shown outcome benefit. Large real datasets now exist (SLICE-3D, >400,000 crops, [Kurtansky 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-03743-w); iToBoS 16,954 region images, [Saha 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05483-x)) and longitudinal lesion matching across meshes reaches 90.1% success at 10 mm on 25K lesion pairs ([Huang 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.103963)); subsurface 3D reconstruction of melanin/blood volumes via Monte Carlo inversion remains a small-sample proof of concept ([D'Alessandro 2012](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2012.2209434), n=14).

'Digital twins' of skin are aspirational: a 2024 systematic search found no direct dermatology applications ([Akbarialiabad 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-024-01220-7)), and the main 2025 contribution is a perspective ([Primiero 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jid.2024.07.011), metadata only). Relevance: high for change detection and lesion localization across visits (a core melanoma surveillance task) and as a source of realistic meshes for physics-based synthetic data (linking to DermSynth3D/S-SYNTH); weak for 'twin' claims that imply mechanistic simulation of lesion evolution, which no retrieved source demonstrates. Gaps: integrating physically based reflectance (Theme 1) into TBP meshes, which currently store only RGB texture, and trials of AI-assisted 3D TBP showing patient-level benefit.

## Limitations of this reference

All takeaways were written from abstracts, and none of the full texts were read. The tables report figures from the derivation studies, which are optimistic relative to independent validation. NICE NG12/NG14, Kittler's revised pattern analysis, Lallas's KA/SCC criteria, Google's Derm Foundation and a dedicated primary GLCM texture paper were searched for but are not included. The 2023–2026 literature on skin microtopography was sparse in PubMed. DermGAN and Sagers 2022 are cited as arXiv preprints. The figure for the Olsen AK thickness grades is metadata-only.
