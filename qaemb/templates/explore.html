II
Structure of the questions

Do questions posed in the same prompt covary?

Each topic was posed as a single prompt. Elevated within-topic correlation admits two explanations: the questions index a shared morphological feature, or the model answers the prompt from a single global impression.

Fig. 2.1Within-topic correlation, against size-matched random groups

Points: mean pairwise Pearson r among a topic's questions, blue where it exceeds the 95th percentile of the null. Bars: 5th to 95th percentile of 1,000 random question sets of equal size. Dashed line: mean between-topic r. Selecting a topic locates it on the question map (Ch. III).

Fig. 2.2Answer correlation as a function of wording similarity

Question pairs binned by cosine similarity of their text embeddings; mean r with 95% interval. Were topic coherence a consequence of shared wording, the two curves would converge.

Box 2.1Competing hypotheses

h1 Feature co-occurrence

The questions of a topic index related structures; any visible vasculature, for instance, raises every vascular item. The answers are partly redundant, but each remains evidence about the lesion.

h2 Prompt-level impression

The model forms one judgement per prompt and propagates it across items. The 63 special-site questions (nail, palmar, facial, mucosal) describe mutually exclusive sites, yet correlate at .

test Stateless re-answering planned

Pose each question in its own prompt on about 20 images and compare within-topic correlations with topic mode (Plan item D).

III
Structure of the questions

How many independent dimensions do the answers span?

Principal component analysis with parallel analysis estimates the number of components exceeding shuffled data; Ward clustering on correlation distance describes what those dimensions contain.

Fig. 3.1Scree plot against parallel analysis

Explained variance per component (log scale). Dashed: 95th percentile from 50 column-wise permutations of the answers.

Fig. 3.2Component variance explained by covariates

η² for categorical covariates, R² for age; shading from 0 to 0.5. Select a row for the highest- and lowest-loading questions.

Fig. 3.3Question clusters and their subtype profiles

Ward clusters of questions, ordered by |AUROC − 0.5| of the cluster mean score for malignant against benign. Cells: cluster mean z-score in each subtype (red above, blue below the mean image). Select a row to list its members.

Clusters correspond to dermoscopic families (vascular patterns, keratinocytic features, pigment network, blue-grey structures) and to the perilesional skin (photodamage, actinic change). Artefact clusters are the least coherent.

Classification

Do the answers discriminate malignant from benign?

L2-penalised logistic regression (ridge) on the z-scored Directional Scores of the 446 clinical questions, compared on identical folds with the model's direct answer to “Is this a malignant lesion?”
