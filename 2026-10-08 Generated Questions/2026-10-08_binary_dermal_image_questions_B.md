# 500 Yes/No Image Questions for Dermal Lesion Characterization

Derived from dermal_lesion_assessment_reference.md and dermal_lesion_sources.csv (131 sources; 88 cited here). Per-question source IDs are in dermal_yes_no_questions.csv. † marks a question that uses standard dermatology or imaging vocabulary not named in the reference (57 questions); these rely on the IDS terminology consensus (Kittler 2016) for vocabulary but the specific criterion is not detailed in the sources. Questions prefixed with a parenthetical condition apply only when that condition holds.

## A. Acquisition and modality (Y001–Y025)
*Main sources: Katragadda 2017; Finnane 2017; Dinnes 2018; Vestergaard 2008; Eapen 2022; Caffery 2018; Nachbar 1994; Henning 2007*

- Y001. Is this a dermoscopic image rather than a macroscopic clinical photograph?
- Y002. Are shiny white structures visible, suggesting the image was taken with polarized dermoscopy?
- Y003. Is there evidence of contact, non-polarized dermoscopy, such as an immersion-fluid layer or contact-plate edge?
- Y004. Does the image appear to come from a smartphone rather than a dedicated dermatoscope or clinical camera?
- Y005. Is the circular field-of-view boundary of a dermatoscope visible?
- Y006. Is the lesion shown at an overview distance that includes recognisable surrounding anatomy?
- Y007. Does the lesion fill more than half of the frame?
- Y008. Does the image look like a tile cropped from a total-body photography system?
- Y009. Is there a specular hotspot or hard shadow indicating on-camera flash?
- Y010. Is surface glare largely absent, consistent with cross-polarized lighting?
- Y011. Is the image from a non-photographic modality (e.g., reflectance confocal mosaic, OCT, ultrasound)?
- Y012. Is the image grayscale or missing full RGB colour?
- Y013. Does the image appear to be a narrow-band, UV, or infrared capture?
- Y014. Is the camera axis roughly perpendicular to the skin surface?
- Y015. Is raking (oblique) light used so that surface relief is exaggerated?
- Y016. Does the frame contain a ruler, scale bar, or size marker? †
- Y017. Is a colour-calibration chart or grey reference card included?
- Y018. Is there a burned-in timestamp, watermark, or text overlay?
- Y019. Do edges look blocky or interpolated, implying the image was upsampled?
- Y020. Are JPEG blocking or ringing artefacts noticeable? †
- Y021. Does the image look digitally sharpened or contrast-boosted?
- Y022. Is there an obvious colour cast (blue, yellow, or green) from incorrect white balance? †
- Y023. Is this a re-photograph of a screen or printout (moiré, visible pixels, paper texture)?
- Y024. Is the lesion approximately centred in the frame?
- Y025. Does the framing suggest a patient-submitted teledermatology photo rather than a standardized clinical capture?
